# Scientific Basis: Gf, Relational Integration, and Transfer

Nov 8, 2026 · Amir

The core of Gf is the ability to quickly build and maintain flexible bindings between elements: at the factor level, relational-processing tasks are almost indistinguishable from Gf. However, neither SMART nor working-memory or relational-integration training has so far produced a reliable increase in Gf in rigorous randomized studies. The goal of the redesign is therefore to maximize the probability of transfer: load the general binding process without loopholes, vary structures and formats, teach structure comparison, and measure transfer honestly on untrained tests.

The audit of Syllogimous v4.1 showed that in 9 of 12 task types the answer is given away by surface cues, and in five types a simple heuristic with no integration at all reaches 97–100% accuracy.

## Architecture of Gf: what counts as the core

Gf is the ability to solve novel problems without relying on learned knowledge; in the CHC model it comprises induction, deduction, and quantitative reasoning, and at the latent level it nearly coincides with the general factor g. Leading models disagree on what exactly limits Gf, but almost all converge on the binding of information in working memory.

| Model of the core | Claim | Key evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relational binding | Gf is the ability to build and maintain stable yet flexible "role–element" bindings | [Chuderski 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence10030051): even a single simple binding can be decisive; [Bateman & Birney 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2019.102893), n = 122: WM–Gf shared variance appears only under random access order, which prevents systematic chunking |
| Relational complexity | The WM limit is set by the number of variables processed in parallel | [Halford et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x98001769); [Halford et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2005.00782.x): 4 variables is the limit, 5 is chance level |
| Maintenance and disengagement | WMC maintains relevant information; Gf disengages from outdated hypotheses | [Shipstead, Harrison & Engle 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616650647); [Burgoyne et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001408): attention control accounts for the shared variance of Gf, WMC, and speed |
| Segmentation | Gf is the ability to break a complex whole into simple, separately processed parts | [Duncan et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1621147114): with a layout that aids segmentation, low-Gf participants improved dramatically |
| Speed of higher-order processes | g is largely the speed of transmission from frontal attention processes to storage | [Schubert et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000325): about 80% of g variance, n = 122 |
| Binding + attention together | Neither relational integration nor attention control alone predicts Gf as well as both together | [Li & Birney 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence14010008), N = 146 |

Neurobiology. Gf relies on a limited fronto-parietal network: in 80 patients, Gf loss was predicted only by lesions of posterolateral frontal, dorsomedial frontal, and parietal cortex ([Woolgar et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1007928107)). Small anatomical changes in this network across development and evolution yield a large leap in relational thinking ([Vendetti & Bunge 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.09.035)).

What the level of Gf depends on:

| Factor | Effect | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Education | +1–5 IQ points per additional year, across all broad abilities, persisting at all ages | [Ritchie & Tucker-Drob 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618774253), >600,000 people |
| Age | In older adults relational integration fails already at medium complexity, especially with distracting information | [Viskontas et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0882-7974.19.4.581) |
| Test-taking strategy | About a third of Raven score gains is explained by a change in scanning strategy; the remainder is non-significant | [Hayes, Petrov & Sederberg 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2014.10.005) |
| Expectations | Self-selection via a suggestive recruitment ad produced the equivalent of +5–10 IQ points after one hour | [Foroughi et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1601243113) |
| Structure of intelligence | If g is a causal higher-order factor, training sub-abilities may not propagate up to g | [Protzko 2017](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1196-1) |

## Relational integration and its link to Gf

Relational integration (RI) is the joint consideration of several relations to reach an inference contained in none of them separately. Among working-memory functions, it is RI that most strongly predicts Gf, and the factor of relational-processing tasks is statistically indistinguishable from the Gf factor.

Relational complexity theory ([Halford, Wilson & Phillips 1998](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x98001769)) defines load as the number of related dimensions that must be held simultaneously: binary, ternary, quaternary relations. People solve more complex tasks through segmentation (step by step) and conceptual chunking (compressing a step's result into a single relation). Real complexity therefore arises only where decomposition is impossible: the chain "A>B, B>C, C>D", when asked about its endpoints, is solved by a series of ternary steps no matter how many premises it has. In tasks where all variables interact, 4 variables is the limit and 5 is chance level ([Halford et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2005.00782.x)).

| Study | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| [Chuderski 2014](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-013-0366-x) | The relation-monitoring task was the strongest predictor of reasoning beyond four types of WM tasks; accuracy depended on the number of bindings, not on the number of objects or on interference |
| [Bateman, Thompson & Birney 2019](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-019-00952-2) | Even a basic version of the task without storage over time explains Gf beyond traditional WM tasks; attentional demands add to the contribution |
| [Jastrzębski, Ociepka & Chuderski 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intell/v82y2020ics0160289620300672.html) | The factor of three relation-verification tasks is statistically equivalent to the Gf factor; they share variance not explained by working memory |
| [Jastrzębski, Ociepka & Chuderski 2023](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-022-01846-z) | Graph Mapping (find the node with the same role in another graph): Gf loading .75, r ≈ .61 with Raven, analogies, and CFT-3, retest .76; difficulty is set by the number of edges, crossings, and "indirect" targets with equal degree |
| [Dauvier, Bailleux & Perret 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036655) | RI levels grow smoothly from age 5 to 12 and are closely linked to Raven |
| [Cho, Holyoak & Cannon 2007](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03193614) | Integrating several relations plus suppressing interference is harder than either alone: the resource is shared |
| [Li & Birney 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence14010008) | RI and attention control predict Gf only jointly, N = 146 |

Neurobiology of RI ([Holyoak & Monti 2021](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_01619)): posterior parietal cortex represents first-order relations, rostrolateral prefrontal cortex (RLPFC) integrates them into higher-order relations, dorsolateral PFC maintains, and ventrolateral PFC suppresses competing information. RLPFC is engaged specifically in the transition from one to two relations, not from zero to one ([Christoff et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2001.0922)).

## What determines binding complexity

RI complexity is set not by the number of premises but by how many relations must be held bound simultaneously and whether the solution can be split into simple steps. A long chain loads memory and the number of steps; irreducible constraints, indeterminacy, and interference load binding itself.

| Factor | Why load increases | How to set it in the trainer | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arity of the solution | More variables in a single act of binding; 4 is a soft limit | Ternary premises ("between"), meta-relations over 4 entities, "from X's point of view" relations | Halford 1998, 2005 |
| Non-decomposability | A chain is solved stepwise with compression; converging branches are not | The inference requires premises not mentioning the query entities; branched structures instead of chains | Halford 2005; Bateman & Birney 2019 |
| Inference depth | More intermediate results | Size of the minimal premise set entailing the answer (σ = 2…6) | "Derivation" dimension in MDML |
| Indeterminacy | All models must be checked, not one "preferred" model; people build one and forget the rest | "Unknown" answer; "possible but not necessary" lures | [Ragni & Knauff 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032460) |
| Premise order | Discontinuous order forces holding unconnected fragments; random access prevents chunking | Random and "discontinuous" order, support premises spread apart | Bateman & Birney 2019 |
| Interference | RI and interference suppression share one resource | Role swaps in the conclusion, similar names, names from the previous task in new roles | Cho 2007; Viskontas 2004 |
| Distractor premises | One must choose what to integrate (segmentation) | Extra premises attached to the same entities | Duncan 2017; Viskontas 2004 |
| Contextual flexibility | Responding against already-bound relations | Arbitrary contextual cues whose meaning switches | [O'Toole & Barnes-Holmes 2009](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/3883/): relational flexibility predicts IQ |
| Multidimensionality | Binding along 2–3 dimensions at once | "Somewhere north" in 2D, position + group in a single task | Halford 1998 |
| Relations between relations | Second-order integration engages RLPFC | Analogies between two derived pairs, structure mapping | Christoff 2001; Holyoak & Monti 2021 |
| Random guessing | With two answers, 50% comes for free and masks under-integration | 3 options, entity choice, or full model construction | Graph Mapping: chance .083 |

Word inversions (red "negations") mainly load text decoding rather than integration — this is my inference from relational complexity theory; there is no direct study. In v5 they therefore remain a separate parameter and do not count as an increase in integration complexity.

## How RFT works and what is known about SMART

RFT describes thinking as the skill of deriving relations that were never taught, and SMART trains this skill with nonsense syllables. The developers' studies reported IQ gains, but two preregistered RCTs from 2026 with active controls found no transfer to Gf.

The mechanism in RFT terms:

- Mutual entailment: "A is bigger than B" entails "B is smaller than A".
- Combinatorial entailment: "A>B" and "B>C" entail "A>C" — this is relational integration.
- Transformation of functions: properties of one stimulus change the meaning of others through the relational network.
- Contextual cues determine which relation applies: coordination, opposition, distinction, comparison, hierarchy, temporal, spatial, conditional and causal, deictic relations (I–you, here–there, now–then).
- New stimuli on every trial, so that the relational pattern is learned rather than specific pairs.

The MDML framework ([Barnes-Holmes et al. 2017](https://contextualscience.org/node/26025)) distinguishes five levels and four dimensions. Levels: mutual entailment → relational frame → relational network → relating relations → relating networks. Dimensions: coherence, complexity, derivation, flexibility. Syllogimous v4.1 works almost entirely at the third level; analogies and meta-relations touch the fourth; the fifth is not covered at all.

SMART: 55 stages, "same/opposite" and "more/less" frames, new syllables on every trial; in school studies children completed on average 15–16 stages over about 12 weeks ([review by Tyndall et al.](https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/6206/1/BPS%20Assessment%20and%20Development%20Tyndall%20-%20Relational%20frame%20theory.pdf)).

| Study | Design | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meijer, Delabie, De Houwer & Cummins 2026, [Collabra](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.158603) | Preregistered RCT, 105 older adults, SMART vs Dual N-back | Relational skills improved; no transfer to progressive matrices or other outcomes |
| Cummins, Meijer, Cassidy & De Houwer 2026, [JCBS](https://contextualscience.org/publications/failure_find_effects_relational_operant_training_relational_abilities_or_scholastic) | Preregistered RCT, 107 children, 18 sessions, SMART vs N-back | No difference in relational skills, Raven, or reading and math |
| Roche et al. 2023, [JCBS](https://contextualscience.org/publications/roche_cummins_cassidy_dillon_moore_grey_2023) | Two school groups without individual randomization | IQ gain disappears when controlling for baseline IQ or attention |
| May et al. 2022, [BJET](https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/id/eprint/6201/) | Meta-analysis: 5 studies, N = 195 | g = 0.57 on nonverbal IQ, but all studies at high risk of bias |
| McLoughlin et al. 2021 | Active control (chess), 30 vs 19 | About +9 points vs ≈ 0 (data from the Tyndall et al. review) |
| McLoughlin et al. 2020 | Active control (programming), 43 vs 27 | About +6 K-BIT-2 points vs less than 2 |
| Cassidy et al. 2011 | Passive control | More than 1 SD (≈ 15 points) on WISC |

The pattern is typical: the stricter the design, the smaller the effect, and in preregistered RCTs with active controls it is zero. This does not prove relational training is useless, but it shows that the SMART format (chains + "true/false") does not by itself produce transfer. The v5 redesign is a theory-driven bet, not a proven method.

## Far transfer: what the data show

With active controls and correction for publication bias, far transfer of cognitive training to Gf is small or zero; robust benefits exist for education and, partly, for long-term reasoning instruction, but not for short computerized trainers.

| Source | Scope | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Gobet & Sala 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221091830) | Review of meta-analyses | Overall far-transfer effect is zero; almost no true difference between training types |
| [Sala et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.203) | Second-order meta-analysis: 99 / 119 / 233 studies | Near transfer exists; far transfer is zero once placebo and bias are controlled |
| [Sala & Gobet 2020](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-019-01681-y) | 41 studies, 2,375 children, WM training | Far transfer with active controls: 0.001 |
| [Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635612) | 87 publications, 145 comparisons | No reliable transfer to nonverbal abilities; WM gains unrelated to far effects |
| [Au et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-014-0699-x) | 20 n-back studies, adults 18–50 | Small but significant effect on Gf; critics attribute it to passive controls |
| [Nguyen, Murphy & Andrews 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000196) | 64 studies, older adults, executive-function training | Effect on Gf disappears after correction for publication bias |
| [Himi et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001328) | RCT, 171 adults, 12 sessions of RI or storage-processing training on one material | Improvement did not transfer even to the same operation with different material |
| [von Bastian et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-012-0280-7) | Training three WM facets, including RI | No transfer to reasoning; training all facets at once yielded less transfer |
| [Pahor, Seitz & Jaeggi 2022](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/06/220620122336.htm) | 3 RCTs, about 500 participants | Transfer to matrices is mediated by near transfer; narrow strategies and dropout block it |
| [Karbach & Kray 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2009.00846.x) | Task-switching training, 3 age groups | Variable training strengthened near transfer in adults; transfer to Gf reported (small samples) |
| [Klauer & Phye 2008](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654307313402) | 74 experiments, about 3,600 children, inductive reasoning | d = 0.52 on Gf, 0.69 on academic learning, effect grew over time; children and many studies by the developers themselves |
| [Mackey et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4141-12.2013); [Guerra-Carrillo & Bunge 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0035-8) | LSAT course (70 hours of reasoning with grouping and ordering rules), quasi-experiment | Strengthened fronto-parietal connectivity, greater gains in visuospatial reasoning than a reading-course control |
| [Wang, Sun & Xiao 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10548-024-01099-3) | RCT, 29 vs 28, one month of RI training | Changes in EEG microstates of the fronto-parietal network; behavioral transfer not assessed in the abstract |
| [Ritchie & Tucker-Drob 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618774253) | 42 datasets, over 600,000 people | +1–5 IQ per year of education — the most reliable known method |

The exceptions share one thing: they are long, varied, and require understanding rules rather than repeating a single format. This is taken as the foundation of the v5 design.

## Mechanisms and neurobiology of transfer

Transfer occurs when the trained and target tasks use the same processes and the skill does not get locked into a narrow routine. Growth of "capacity" is barely demonstrated; growth of efficiency (strategies, automatization) is demonstrated, so the trainer's goal is to make only general strategies pay off.

| Mechanism | Essence | Implication for the trainer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared processes | Transfer to 3-back occurred only via shared striatal activation ([Dahlin et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1155466)); primitive operations on information transfer, not content ([Taatgen 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0033138)) | Train what Gf tests demand: binding, integration, hypothesis switching, structure mapping |
| Efficiency, not capacity | Transfer, if any, comes through efficiency gains ([von Bastian et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-021-00001-3.pdf)) | Target general model-building strategies and automatization of basic inferences |
| Cognitive routines | Training builds new routines that work only in tasks of the same structure ([Gathercole et al. 2019](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/working-memory-training-involves-learning-new-skills/)) | Break narrow routines through variability and interleaving |
| Supply–demand mismatch | Plasticity is triggered by prolonged mismatch, but a strategy can absorb it ([Lövdén et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020080)) | A loophole absorbs the load without adaptation — it must be closed |
| Near transfer as a gateway | Far transfer is mediated by near transfer (Pahor et al. 2022) | Measure transfer to untrained formats of the same process |
| Comparison and abstraction | Case comparison: d = 0.50; searching for similarities + principle after comparison: d = 1.18 ([Alfieri et al. 2013](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/ContrastingCasesMeta-AlfieriEtAl2013.pdf)) | Structure-mapping mode and post-answer explanation |
| Interleaving | Interleaving instead of blocks: g = 0.42, stronger for similar categories and complex material ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209)) | No runs of the same type in a row |
| Variability | Variable input slows initial learning but yields a more general skill ([Raviv, Lupyan & Green 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2022.03.007)) | Vary form, wording, and answer format while keeping the same structure |

Neurobiology. Training primarily engages general learning networks — the dorsal attention network, the salience network, and the striatum — and transfer to similar tasks runs through the fronto-striatal system ([Salmi, Nyberg & Laine 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.03.019)). Working-memory training increases prefrontal neuronal activity and prefrontal–parietal connectivity, modulated by dopamine ([Constantinidis & Klingberg 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.43)). 70 hours of a reasoning course change white matter between the frontal lobes ([Mackey et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2012.00032)) and strengthen resting-state fronto-parietal connectivity. At the same time, the expansion–renormalization model shows that gray matter first grows during skill acquisition and then returns to baseline, so neural changes during training do not equal a lasting increase in ability ([Wenger et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2017.09.008)).

What kills transfer: surface cues, blocks of a single type, a single answer format, rewarding speed when guessing is viable, tasks that are too easy or impossible, low engagement.

## Conditions for maximal transfer

No conditions guarantee transfer; below are the necessary ones, without which transfer is practically ruled out by theory and data, and the desirable ones that raise its chances. The strength of evidence is stated honestly: no single RCT has directly tested the whole set.

| Condition | Type | Why | Evidence | In v5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The answer cannot be obtained without integration | Necessary | A template absorbs the load and builds a narrow routine | Theory + correlational data (Lövdén 2010; Gathercole 2019) | "Answer × cue profile" stratification + heuristic bots as tests |
| Integration is irreducible to local premises | Necessary | Flexible binding is precisely what WM and Gf share | Strong correlational (Bateman & Birney 2019; Halford 2005) | The minimal support contains a premise without the query entities |
| Attention control within integration | Necessary | Gf is predicted by RI and attention only jointly | Moderate (Li & Birney 2026; Cho 2007) | Lures, role swaps, distractors, similar names |
| Multi-model reasoning | Necessary | Gf also means disengaging from a wrong hypothesis | Moderate (Shipstead 2016; Ragni & Knauff 2013) | "Unknown" answer, "possible but not necessary" lures |
| Variability of structures, frames, and formats | Necessary | Training on one material does not transfer even to the same operation | Moderate (Himi 2023; Raviv 2022; Karbach & Kray 2009) | 15 frames, 4 answer formats, varied topology |
| Interleaving of types | Desirable | Forces recognition of the task's structure | Strong for learning (Brunmair & Richter 2019) | Scheduler without blocks |
| Adaptive difficulty around 75% correct | Desirable | Mismatch without failure | Theory (Lövdén 2010); adaptivity itself not proven as a cause of transfer | Weighted staircase per frame |
| Structure comparison with post-hoc explanation | Desirable | Abstraction of the schema from the surface | Strong for learning (Alfieri 2013); Graph Mapping loads Gf at .75 | "Structure Mapping" mode |
| Explicit general strategies and instruction on transferring them | Desirable | The skill does not get stuck in one format | Moderate (von Bastian 2022) | Strategy cards and error explanations |
| Monitoring near transfer | Desirable | Without it there is no far transfer | Moderate (Pahor 2022) | "Transfer Check" section |
| Dose and spacing | Desirable | Successful cases involve dozens of hours over months | Indirect (Mackey 2012, 2013; Klauer & Phye 2008) | 25–35 min per day, 8–12 weeks |
| Engagement | Desirable | Dropout blocks near transfer | Moderate (Pahor 2022) | Short sessions, no speed rewards in deep modes |

A separate note on "pure computing power". Strategy formation cannot be bypassed: any training works mainly through strategies. The design task is to make only general strategies pay off (build a single model, check alternatives, pick the decisive relations), while narrow templates yield chance-level accuracy.

## Syllogimous v4.1 audit: templates found

I ran the v4.1 generators themselves and, on 1,200 items per cell, tested "bots" that see only the on-screen text and do not integrate premises. Chance with two answer options is 50%; anything noticeably above it is a loophole the brain will sooner or later find on its own.

| Type | Loophole | Accuracy without integration | Cause in code |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Same / opposite | Count "opposite": odd → opposite | 100% (66–74% with meta-relations) | Premises are always a simple chain; the conclusion is always about its endpoints |
| Number and time comparison | Each word's "win count" across premises | 100% with 2–3 premises, 85% with 5, 66% with 12 | Always a chain; chain endpoints are visible locally |
| Syllogisms, "Canyon" | Conclusion "All…" is valid in 1–8% of cases, "Some… are not…" in 72–76% | 74–80% from the quantifier alone; atmosphere effect 70–78% | Invalid conclusions are built by a different path than valid ones |
| Syllogisms, "Fredo" | Same quantifier loophole | 62–70% | Classic syllogism + noise |
| Linear and circular order | The line "… are arranged in a row" lists items in their true order | 100% without reading the premises | `formatArrangementInstruction(words)` receives the array in position order |
| 2D directions | "In the adjacent cell" in the conclusion is always true | 100% on 19–31% of items; 59–70% overall | When "corrupting" the conclusion, `adjacent = false` is set |
| 2D directions with inversions | Red diagonal when only one axis is inverted — text is ambiguous (a bug, not a loophole) | 58% of items contain such a phrase; an exact text-based solver gets 82–87% | `formatDirectionRelationship` colors the whole diagonal |
| 3D directions | More steps than the grid size → always false; "same point" → always true | 0% and 100% valid in these subgroups | The "+1 step" distortion goes out of bounds |
| Graph comparison | Compare vertex degrees | 97–100% (without meta-relations) | The distorted graph almost always changes degrees |
| Analogies on order | "Same" with 5+ premises is almost always false | 11–31% valid vs 70–88% for "different" | No balancing |
| Logical connectives | XOR and XNOR are more often true | 59–60% valid | Weak balancing |

Remedy test. A prototype generator for ordering with "greater" and "between" premises showed that answer balancing alone is insufficient: the "win count" bot stayed at 66–79% against 33% chance. When the generator first chooses a pair "answer × expected bot opinion", then builds premises with false trails and requires that the conclusion cannot be derived from the premises containing the query words themselves, all three bots dropped to 28–38%, with median generation time of 0.2–3 ms. This method is built into the Claude Code prompt.

## Training and measurement protocol

Working plan: 25–35 minutes per day, 5–6 days per week, 8–12 weeks, with measurements on untrained tests before, after, and one month later. The level within the trainer itself is not evidence of Gf growth.

1. Week 0: two measurements one week apart with no training, to measure the pure retest effect.
2. Tests — at least two different Gf formats, in alternate forms: normed [Sandia Matrices](https://github.com/sandialabs/Matrices) (split into non-overlapping sets) and a graph-mapping test in the style of [Graph Mapping](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-022-01846-z); plus any number- or letter-series test. These must not be trained.
3. Identical testing conditions: time of day, sleep the night before, no more caffeine than usual, the same device.
4. Training for 8–12 weeks: roughly 50% mixed frames with three answer options, 25% "Constructor", 15% "Structure Mapping", 10% "Fluency". The dose is comparable to successful reasoning courses: about 70 hours over 3 months.
5. Inside the trainer, every 14 days — "Transfer Check": formats that are never trained. If there is no gain there within 4–6 weeks, far transfer should not be expected (per Pahor et al. 2022) — change the mode mix.
6. After the course: measure immediately and again after 4 weeks without training.

How to read the result: subtract the week-0 gain from the course gain; a gain of less than 5–7 IQ points is indistinguishable from expectation and test strategy (Foroughi 2016; Hayes 2015). Gains on only one of the two tests point to strategy rather than Gf.

## Gaps in the evidence

The main gap: there is no rigorous RCT in which relational-integration training with the properties described raised latent Gf in adults.

- Growth of binding "capacity" has not been shown in any study; growth of efficiency in trained tasks has.
- Data on variability are contradictory: variable task-switching training helped, while training three WM facets at once produced less transfer (von Bastian 2013). The optimal degree of variability is unknown.
- The positive cases are methodologically weak: the LSAT course was not randomized, Klauer involves children and largely the author's own lab, Wang 2025 has a small sample and a neural rather than behavioral outcome.
- Neural changes (connectivity, EEG microstates) do not prove cognitive growth and may renormalize.
- It is unclear how much of any transfer could come from the speed of basic inferences (fluency) versus depth of integration.
- The effect of inversions and other linguistic complications on transfer has not been studied directly.
- In a single-person self-experiment, expectations and retesting cannot be fully separated; the protocol above only reduces the error.
